# Cross Domain Time Series Foundation Models

## Abstract
The emergence of Time Series Foundation Models (TSFMs) promises a shift toward universal, zero-shot forecasting capabilities. However, their effectiveness across diverse domains remains an open question. In this study, we conduct a rigorous geometric audit of leading TSFMs (TIMESFM, MOIRAI, Wav2Vec 2.0, ECGFounder, Astromer, FinCast) across four distinct domains: Finance (S&P 500 stocks), Healthcare (PTB-XL), Audio (ESC-50), and Astronomy (StarEmbed). By deploying a multi-head classification suite, ranging from logistic regression to multi-layer perceptrons, we benchmark these large-scale models against strict random initializations and lightweight, domain-specific features. Our results reveal a critical "Stochasticity Barrier": while TSFMs demonstrate strong positive transfer in deterministic, physical domains, they fail to significantly outperform random noise in high-entropy financial environments (Accuracy \approx 52%). Furthermore, we observe that domain-specific foundation models do not consistently outperform generalist architectures. Notably, lightweight custom feature extractors frequently rival or outperform massive pre-trained models, achieving 90.3% accuracy in astronomy tasks where TSFMs capped at 67.9%, challenging the assumption that scale is the only path to performance. We conclude that current pre-training objectives successfully encode morphological and periodic laws but struggle to disentangle stochastic signal from noise, necessitating a re-evaluation of zero-shot transfer in non-stationary domains.
